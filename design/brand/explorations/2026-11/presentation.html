Identity exploration · November 2026

Code becomes action. Ten ideas for the moment written intent turns into a precise, permitted act.

Every symbol was drawn in solid black first, on a 64-unit grid with an 8-unit margin. No letters, arrows, brackets or code characters; no gradients or effects.

The three finalists were redrawn for 24 and 16 pixels, tested in white on black, set with the wordmark, and given one signature colour each. The pixel tests below are true-size renders, magnified.

Ten directions

Symbol concepts

Each is a different idea, not a variation. The small marks beside each symbol are the same drawing at 24 and 16 px; where a concept failed at that size or read as something else, the note says so.

01Finalist

Indent

Three lines of code, each indented one step deeper, fuse on the right into one quarter-turn: structure on the left, a single motion on the right.

DistinctiveA stepped inner edge against one clean arc.

02Finalist

Heron

The heron stands still, reads the water, and strikes once, exactly: calm attention, then one precise act. The kink in its neck is the strike held in reserve until it is released.

DistinctiveA kinked neck under a long, level bill.

03Finalist

Half-turn

One ribbon, one half-turn. The outlined face is the plan; after the turn the same ribbon shows its solid face: the act. Nothing is lost in translation.

DistinctiveOutline meets solid at a single crossing point.

04

Cam

A cam turns rotation into a straight stroke: the circular work of reasoning, converted into one exact movement of the follower above it.

DistinctiveAn off-centre lobe with a shaft hole, and a detached follower.

Why notStrong idea, but at 16 px the lobe and follower read as a letter d.

05

Tumbler

Pin stacks of different lengths are the key's code; when they are right, every split meets the shear line and the lock turns.

DistinctiveIrregular columns cut by one perfectly straight line.

Why notThe meaning is exact; the silhouette reads as an audio equaliser.

06

Pawl

A rack and pawl advance one tooth at a time and never slip back: each step approved and kept.

DistinctiveA sawtooth base with one hooked catch.

Why notToo many parts; the teeth read as a factory roofline.

07

Hinge

Two leaves joined on one axis by interleaved knuckles; one has begun to swing open.

DistinctiveAn interleaved seam between unequal leaves.

Why notThe interleaved seam reads as the letter E.

08

Dovetail

A dovetail joint: intent and execution cut to fit each other, holding without a fastener.

DistinctiveA flared tenon locked into its socket.

Why notCollapses into a door or a pair of panels below 32 px.

09

First tile

One tile is released and the rest follow in a known order: a single approval, a deterministic chain.

DistinctiveTwo standing slabs and one leaning into them.

Why notThe leaning tile reads as a slash, a coding character.

10

Ejected line

One line is pushed out of the page and becomes a solid object beside it.

DistinctiveA slotted plate with its missing piece outside.

Why notReads as a document or menu icon: generic interface vocabulary.

Recommendation

Indent

Indent says the brand in its construction: indentation is the most recognisable trace of code, and here it resolves into one arc. It is the only finalist that is unambiguous at 16 px, it has no part that reads as a letter, and its silhouette (stepped inside, swept outside) has no near neighbour among developer tools. Heron is the most characterful; Half-turn the most conceptual.
